## Before Pitch Day (11/9-11/16)

The most important job this week is finding your data and opening it in Python. Projects most often get stuck on data, not on code.

By the start of lab on **Mon 11/16**:
1. Pick 2-3 candidate questions. A good question is specific enough that one figure could answer it: "Has the rate of glacier retreat at X sped up since 2000?" rather than "How is climate change affecting glaciers?"
2. Find a dataset for at least one of them that we haven't analyzed in class (see "What counts as new data" in the Final Project Guide). Write down exactly where it came from: the specific page, not just the agency's home page.
3. Load it in Python and look at it: `df.head()`, `df.shape`, the column names, the units, and any fill values like `-9999`.
4. Bring it to the lab. You can paste what you found into your `proposal.md` file.

If the first dataset doesn't work out, that's useful to know now. Have a backup in mind.

### When you're sharing
1. Your question in one sentence
2. Your data: what it is, where it's from, and `df.head()` and `df.shape` (at least) on screen
3. Where your uncertainty will come from

### When you listen
Ask 2-3 questions. Good things to think about:
- Is the question specific enough, or too broad?
- Can they finish this in about 15 hours of work?
- Is there a number that could answer their question? where will their uncertainty come from?
- What happens if the data don't show what they expect?

Good feedback asks real questions, points out challenges constructively, and shares datasets or ideas you know of. It doesn't call a project "bad", tell someone to change topics entirely, or get stuck on small details.
